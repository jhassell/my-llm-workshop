Page 3: Step-by-Step Procedure

1. Drain the Old Oil

  1. Loosen the drain plug. 14 mm socket, turn counterclockwise. It may be tight — use a breaker bar if needed.
  2. Remove by hand. Once loose, unscrew the last few turns with your fingers. Keep pressure on the plug so it doesn't fall into the pan.
  3. Let it drain completely. 5–10 minutes. Watch the stream slow to a drip. Wipe the plug and pan surface clean.
  4. Inspect the old oil. Glitter = metal (bad). Milky = coolant leak (very bad). Gas smell = fuel dilution.
  5. Replace the crush washer. Slide the new copper/aluminum washer onto the plug. Old one stays on the plug — discard both together.
  6. Reinstall drain plug. Thread by hand to avoid cross-threading. Torque to 25–33 ft-lb (34–45 N·m). Do not overtighten — the oil pan is aluminum.

2. Replace the Oil Filter (Cartridge Style)

  1. Locate the filter housing. Front of engine, driver's side, black metal canister with a 19 mm hex cap.
  2. Position a rag under the housing. Oil will spill when you open it.
  3. Unscrew the cap. 19 mm socket on ratchet. Turn counterclockwise. The cartridge comes out with the cap.
  4. Remove old cartridge. Pull it off the cap. It's dirty — let it drain into the pan.
  5. Remove old O-rings. Two O-rings: one large on the cap, one small on the stem. Pick them off with a pick or fingernail.
  6. Install new O-rings. Coat lightly with clean oil. Large ring seats in the cap groove; small ring goes on the stem.
  7. Install new cartridge. Press it onto the cap — it only fits one way (center post aligns).
  8. Reinstall cap assembly. Thread by hand. Torque to ~15 ft-lb (20 N·m). Do not overtighten — the housing cracks easily.

3. Refill with New Oil

  1. Double-check: Drain plug tight? Filter cap tight? Skid plate back on?
  2. Lower the truck. Remove jack stands, lower to ground. Level ground matters for accurate fill.
  3. Add oil. Funnel in filler hole. Pour in 5 quarts initially. Replace filler cap.
  4. Start engine. Run 10–15 seconds. Oil pressure light should go out within 3 seconds.
  5. Check for leaks. Look at drain plug and filter housing. No drips = good.
  6. Shut off. Wait 2 minutes. Oil settles in the pan.
  7. Check dipstick. Pull, wipe, reinsert fully, pull again. Level should be between the two holes (L and H).
  8. Top off. Add the remaining ~0.4 quarts in small increments, rechecking each time. Do not overfill.

4. Cleanup

  • Wipe any spills — oil on the exhaust smokes and smells.
  • Pour old oil from drain pan into the empty oil bottles (funnel helps). Cap them.
  • Bag the old filter and crush washer.
  • Take used oil & filter to any auto parts store or recycling center. Never pour down a drain or on the ground.
